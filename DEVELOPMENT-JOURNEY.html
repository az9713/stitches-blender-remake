1. The whole project in one page

RECONSTRUCTED The user wanted a 31-second vertical video that shows a stitched cut on a leg healing. The reference video does this in one continuous shot. The camera pushes in on the wound, holds, pushes in further to show the tissue stages, then pulls out to a thin scar. The user chose to make a new video with the same beats, but with new 3D assets, new words, and a new voice. He did not choose to copy frames or audio (HANDOFF.md, "the user's decisions (2026-11-03)").

No AI video generator made any picture. Every frame is a real 3D render. A Python script builds the scene in Blender. A second script renders it. A third script grades the colour. ffmpeg joins the picture and the sound.

3. Stage 1: measure the reference

RECONSTRUCTED LIVE (the script ran again this session)

The tutorial's central idea is this: do not guess what the reference looks like. Measure every frame, then build to the measured numbers. tools/measure.py reads a video frame by frame at 256x456 pixels. For each frame it records:

  • Brightness (mean grey level, 0 to 255), contrast (standard deviation of grey level), and saturation (mean of the HSV S channel).
  • Sideways motion in pixels per frame. It uses phase correlation: it compares the frequency content of two frames to find how far the picture slid.
  • Zoom. It converts the frame spectrum to log-polar coordinates. A zoom becomes a slide there, so phase correlation reads it. The product of the per-frame zooms gives the cumulative zoom.
  • The colour of the top-left corner, which shows the blue background in wide shots.

The findings are in beats.md: no hard cuts; 31.031 s; 1296x2304; 930 frames at 30000/1001 fps; zoom 1.00 at 0 s, 1.43 at 4 s, 2.94 at 23 s, and 0.57 at 30 s; loudness -12.0 LUFS, loudness range 1.6 LU, true peak +1.6 dBFS.

Decisions and traps in this stage

  • Measured loudness against the transcript. The tutorial says "around minus 5 LUFS". The downloaded file measures -12.0 LUFS. The project uses -12.0. Rule: trust your own measurement of the file in front of you over a number somebody said.
  • cv2 seeking is wrong on this file. OpenCV cannot jump to a frame in this AV1 video and lands on the wrong frames. Every script decodes in order, from frame 1.
  • The zoom number is too small. Phase correlation under-reads the push-in by about a power of 2. The camera distances in DIST (build_scene.py) were calibrated on the spacing of the stitches in reference frames instead of on the zoom column.

4. Stage 2: build the 3D scene

RECONSTRUCTED from tools/build_scene.py (869 lines, read in full this session). It runs as blender -b --python tools/build_scene.py -- build/scene.blend. It has options --cuts 23 and --hair 12000. The file is one script on purpose.

4.1 The human

build_human() asks MPFB for a male body with these macro values: muscle 0.88, weight 0.62, proportions 0.7, height 0.55, age 0.5, caucasian 1.0. It adds the default rig with weights. It then deletes all geometry above z = 1.08 m or more than 0.30 m from the centre line, because the camera never sees the upper body. It also removes vertex groups and modifiers that the video does not need. Less geometry means a smaller, faster scene.

4.2 The wound is the skin, not a sticker

The most important design decision is in the docstring of build_patch(): "The wound IS the skin: nothing floats above it, so no triangulation poke-through (transcript's bug)." The tutorial's method put a separate wound patch over the skin, and the skin showed through it. This build avoids that:

  1. It subdivides the calf skin around the cut (23 cuts per edge) to get enough vertices.
  2. It writes six shape keys (saved vertex shapes that Blender can blend): open, closed, swell, gap, ridge, scar. Each key moves vertices along the skin normal by a few millimetres (for example closed = -4.2 mm).
  3. It writes nine vertex attributes (a_g_open, a_lobe, a_fringe, a_vd, a_vt, and others). These are per-vertex masks that the shader reads to paint colour on the correct region.
  4. The function field(u, v) defines every shape with smooth-step maths. u is distance across the cut. v is position along it from -1 to 1. The cut is 2 x 0.066 m long and 0.37 m up the leg (ZC, HALF_L). It is wavy (centre()) and tapers at both ends (taper()).
  5. vessel_segments() grows branching lines with a fixed random seed (11). Each segment has a growth order. The material reveals them in that order, so vessels appear to grow.

The healing story is then only keyframes. STAGE_KEYS lists seconds and values for each shape key. For example, open goes 1 until 2.6 s and 0 at 5.0 s. swell starts rising at 8 s and peaks at 15.5 s. scar appears at 26.8 s. The shader has matching timed values (k_inf, k_yel, k_v, k_grow, k_tis, k_scar).

4.3 Skin material

skin_material() builds one node graph. It mixes layers in this order: base tone with large and fine noise; pale patch; pore dots (Voronoi texture); hair-follicle dashes stretched along the leg; orange fringe then red lips (inflammation); dark brown-red ridged tissue; yellow scaffold in the gap; bright orange-red vessels; the open wound; the dark closed line; the scar. The surface is a Principled BSDF with subsurface scattering (weight 0.45, scale 0.004, radius 0.9/0.35/0.18) and roughness 0.5 plus noise. A bump node (strength 0.42, distance 1.1 mm) adds pores. Subsurface scattering lets light travel a little inside the skin. Without it, skin looks like painted plastic.

4.4 Hair: curves, not particles

The first try used Blender's particle hair. The handoff records the result: the hair came out about 10 cm long, and one 38 s test frame spent 37 s on hair. The final add_hair() writes 12,000 short hair curves directly (3 control points each, 3 to 6 mm long, radius 0.06 mm at the root). It picks root points by triangle area, weighted so that hair stops near the wound and ends at z = 0.505 m. Hair is parented to the shin bone. It does not move on its own.

4.5 Stitches, thread, needle, scissors

  • 12 stitches (STITCH_TIMES) land one per 7.5 frames, as the transcript says (6 to 8 frames). Each draws itself over 7 frames by animating bevel_factor_end from 0 to 1.
  • Each stitch is a curve with a swell shape key, so the thread rises with the swelling skin. Its thickness animates, then thins and vanishes at 24.6 to 26.4 s.
  • A slack loop on the camera-right side pulls tight between 3.2 and 5.0 s (shape key taut). The loops hide at 10 s.
  • The needle is a thin steel cylinder, visible from 2.8 s to 5.84 s. The scissors are two blades, two rings, and a rivet. They pop in open at 5.27 s, close at 5.84 s, and leave at 6.2 s. Those times were measured on the reference.

4.6 Clothes

shell() copies the body between two heights and pushes it out along the normals. That gives shorts (from z = 0.610 m up), a blue sock (0.055 to 0.128 m), and a shoe (below 0.096 m). It is cheap and it follows the leg through the walk. It is also the reason the shoe is boxy: it is an inflated copy of the foot, not a modelled shoe. This is the first item in "known gaps" (section 14).

4.7 The walk

The man walks on a treadmill, so the leg swings but does not move forward. build_walk() keys six bones every 4 frames on a sine wave with a 5.0 s period. A 5.0 s period makes the pose at 0 s equal the pose at 30 s, as in the reference. pin_wound() then moves the whole armature so the wound keeps only 22 % (keep=0.22) of its natural sway. That matches the measured 0.447 px/frame of sideways motion.

4.8 Camera, focus, light

  • One camera moves on one straight line to the wound (axis = (0.10, -1.0, -0.30) normalised). DIST lists the distance in metres at set times: 0.73 m at 0 s, 0.30 m at 4.2 s, 0.105 m at 22 s, 1.20 m at 31.1 s. The curves are Bezier with auto-clamped handles.
  • Lens 50 mm, sensor height 36 mm, clip_start 0.005 m. The default clip distance of 0.1 m cut the leg open at the 0.105 m macro distance. The fix cost a debug cycle (handoff).
  • Depth of field follows an empty object parented to the wound. The f-stop is 3.2 to 3.6 early (soft background), 8.0 at 16 to 24.5 s (more in focus), then 3.2 again.
  • One sun lamp (energy 4.6, 2 degree angle, warm colour) from upper left and front, plus a pale blue world light at strength 0.26. The sun puts the right edge of the leg in shade, as in the reference.

6. Stage 4: sound

RECONSTRUCTED The narration is 82 words in five parts (beats.md), at a target of 2.6 words per second. ElevenLabs made voice, music, and four sound effects. Every asset in use is saved in audio/. The files with suffix _a and _b are two takes from one call (default two takes). Their hashes differ and their lengths are equal. Take A is in use.

tools/mix.py runs one ffmpeg filter graph:

  1. Cut voice take A at its measured silences into six clips, place each at a set time with a 20 ms fade in and a 40 ms fade out.
  2. Music at -15 dB, trimmed to 31.031 s, 1.5 s fade out.
  3. Needle sound at -20 dB on each stitch landing (the times come from the same formula as STITCH_TIMES). Snip at -8 dB at 5.80 s. Whoosh at -14 dB at 13.6 s and 24.7 s. Tissue at -22 dB at 15, 17, 19, 21, 23 s. silenceremove trims lead-in silence so each effect lands on its time.
  4. Mix, then compress (threshold -22 dB, ratio 3, 4 dB make-up), then loudnorm to -12 LUFS, then a limiter at 0.9.

Result in version 1 and version 2 (same file): LIVE -12.6 LUFS integrated, loudness range 2.5 LU (ffmpeg -af ebur128 on the final MP4, measured this session). Reference: -12.0 LUFS, 1.6 LU.

Nobody has listened to the mix. The agent can measure sound, but it cannot hear it. The numbers say the loudness is near the reference. They do not say the voice is clear over the music, that the effects land on the right picture, or that the voice sounds good.

7. Stage 5: finish the picture and encode

LIVE tools/post.py (97 lines, read in full) takes each rendered PNG and makes the final frame:

  1. Upscale 1080x1920 to 1296x2304 with Lanczos interpolation.
  2. Grade the foreground: raise the colours to the power GAMMA (a number below 1 makes the picture brighter), then scale the distance from grey by SAT.
  3. Composite over a vertical blue gradient. The gradient colours are samples from the left edge of the reference at 0 s (G0) and 30 s (G30), 11 points each, blended by frame number. The PNG has straight alpha, so the maths is fg*a + bg*(1-a).
  4. Blur 0.9 px (softer lens), add a glow from pixels above 0.75 (blur 28 px, weight 0.7), multiply a vignette (18 % at the corners), add Gaussian grain of strength GRAIN.
  5. Send raw frames to ffmpeg through a pipe. Encode H.264, yuv420p, CRF 20, preset medium, +faststart. Audio is AAC 256 kbps.

post.py also has a sheet mode that puts reference and render side by side. That produced build/compare_latest.png in an earlier session.

12. Unknown unknowns

These are things that a person new to this domain does not know to ask about. Each has a short answer and a "what to do".

12.1 About measuring

The numbers do not measure realism. measure.py reads brightness, contrast, saturation, motion, and zoom. Version 2 is close on those. A video can match all five and still have bad skin, a plastic shoe, or a floating thread. The handoff already lists three such faults (shoe, hair, calf).

What to do: look at frames side by side with python tools/post.py sheet build/frames out.png 100 400 700, and watch the whole film at full size.

Fitting to a metric. The grade came from a grid search on 30 frames. A search will always find the best pair for the metric you give it. It fits those 30 frames, not "looks right". The match on the other 900 frames is shown by the whole-film numbers (they agree: 129.1 against 132.4), which is good evidence but still one set of three numbers.

Averages hide spikes. The mean sideways motion went from 0.44 to 0.62 because of one second. A mean over 930 frames can look fine with a bad second. Read the per-second table in build/measure_v2.txt as well.

A measurement tool has its own errors. Phase correlation fails at fast zoom, and cv2 seeks wrongly in AV1. Three facts in this project came from a failing instrument, not from the video. Check the instrument first when a number looks extreme.

12.2 About the 3D and render

Why Cycles, and why it is slow. Cycles traces light paths with random sampling. Fewer samples give noisy images, so the denoiser (OptiX) cleans the noise. The average of 22.7 s per frame is for a 1080x1920 image at a low sample count on a GPU. More samples or the CPU would take far longer.

Why a geometry change costs 5.4 hours. The picture is rendered once. A colour change can run after the render (cheap). A shape change needs all 930 frames again (the handoff estimates 5.4 hours at 48 spp). This is why the grade lives in post.py and not in the scene.

Alpha and the background. The scene renders with no background. The blue sky is added later from measured colours. This gives an exact match to the blue but removes any colour bounce from a real sky onto the leg. The reference does not show that bounce clearly, so it was a fair trade. I have not tested the alternative.

Display-referred and scene-referred. Blender stores light as linear numbers, and the "view transform" maps them to screen colours. post.py works on the screen colours after that map. A different view transform changes the whole look and the grade values would need a new search.

Why a human mesh from MPFB. It is free and rigged. The handoff lists the cost: the shoe is a shell, the hair stops at 0.505 m, and the calf is less bulged than the reference.

12.3 About video and sound files

CRF and size. CRF is the quality dial of x264. A lower number gives higher quality and a bigger file. Version 1 (CRF 16, preset slow) is 164.9 MB. Version 2 (CRF 20, preset medium) is 19.9 MB. I did not compare the two files by eye. The size ratio is 8.3 times. Many sites (YouTube, TikTok) encode your upload again, so a very large source file has limited value.

29.97 against 30. The frame rate is 30000/1001, not 30. A wrong rate drifts the sound against the picture by about 1 frame every 33 seconds. Everything here keeps 30000/1001.

Why -shortest dropped a frame in version 1. The audio is 31.000 s and the video 31.031 s. -shortest stops at the shorter stream. Version 2 does not use it, and the audio ends one frame early. One frame of silence at the end is not audible.

LUFS. It measures loudness as the ear does, over the whole file. The reference is at -12.0. Version 2 is at -12.6. A 0.6 LU gap is small. Streaming sites often normalise loudness anyway. The reference's true peak is +1.6 dBFS, which is above full scale. Our limiter keeps peaks below full scale (-0.8 dBFS in version 1).

12.4 About process

The agent cannot hear, and it sees only if it opens an image. Most of its checks are numbers. This session I opened no image. If you need a judgement of look or sound, that is your job or a dedicated review step.

Background jobs can be stopped. Claude Code can end a background shell under memory pressure. The renderer resumes by skipped PNGs, but post.py does not resume. Design long jobs so that a stop costs little.

Estimates age. See problem 1 in section 10.

Choice of model and effort. Opus high for judgement and geometry work, Sonnet low for command running and reporting. The handoff says an advisor model was consulted once before the build. I used no subagent and no advisor in this session. I do not have a measured comparison of cost or quality between models for this project.

No version control. The project is not a git repository. There is one backup of the scene (build/scene.blend1, from Blender's own save). A disk fault or a bad overwrite would lose the work. The tools folder (5 scripts) is the smallest thing to protect, because the scene and frames can be rebuilt from it. This is a recommendation. I have not acted on it.

12.5 About rights, money, and accuracy

Imitation of a living channel's format. The user chose same beats, own words, new assets, no reused frames or audio. That removes direct copying. It does not remove the question of how a viewer or a platform sees a video that copies the structure and look of another creator's popular short. I have no legal view on this and I did not research it. Consider crediting the format's source if you publish.

Licences of the tools. I did not check the licence terms for MPFB output, the ElevenLabs voice and music on your plan, or the Omi tutorial's paid skill (it was not used). Check them before commercial use. NOT KNOWN

Medical content. The narration says a deep cut is stitched, swells as cells clear damage, then collagen forms a scaffold, then new vessels grow, then stitches come out and a thin scar remains. This follows the reference's order and is simplified. No clinician reviewed it. The stitch-removal timing and the depiction of tissue colours are artistic. Do not present the video as medical teaching without a review.

Money. ElevenLabs: 8,843 credits, about $1.47 (voice 842, music 7,200, effects 802). The GPU render used electricity for about 5.85 h. Claude Code usage cost is not recorded in the files. NOT KNOWN
